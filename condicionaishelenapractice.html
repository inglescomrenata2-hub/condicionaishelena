Três tipos de situação, três estruturas
Todos os conditionals têm duas partes: a condição (if...) e o resultado. O que muda é o quão real ou imaginária é a situação.
Zero Conditional — fatos e protocolos
If + present simple, present simple

Usamos para algo que sempre acontece: fatos médicos, regras, protocolos de rotina. Dá para trocar if por when.

  • If a patient has a fever, we check for signs of infection.
  • When blood pressure drops, the heart beats faster.
First Conditional — possibilidade real no futuro
If + present simple, will + verbo base

Usamos para algo que pode realmente acontecer e qual será a consequência. Atenção: nunca use will logo depois do if.

  • If the troponin level is high, we will call the cardiology team.
  • Unless the pain improves, we will transfer him to the ICU. (unless = if ... not)
Second Conditional — situações imaginárias e conselhos
If + past simple, would + verbo base

Usamos para situações imaginárias, improváveis ou contrárias à realidade — e também para dar conselhos com If I were you.... O passado aqui não indica tempo passado: indica distância da realidade.

  • If I were the attending, I would order an angiography.
  • If I were you, I would ask for a second opinion.
  • Com o verbo to be, o padrão é were para todas as pessoas (was aparece no inglês informal).
TipoEstruturaQuando usarExemplo clínico
Zero present + present Fatos, regras, protocolos If oxygen saturation drops, we give oxygen.
First present + will Possibilidade real no futuro If the ECG is abnormal, we will repeat it.
Second past + would Imaginário, improvável, conselho If we had more beds, we would admit him today.
💡 Teste rápido: É um fato ou uma regra? → Zero. Pode realmente acontecer? → First. É imaginário ou improvável? → Second.
🩺 Conectando com o que você já estudou: no resultado, você pode trocar will / would por modais como might, could e should. Ex.: If the ECG is normal, we might still need imaging. / If I were you, I should call the cardiologist.

Case Study Speaking: apresentando um caso clínico
Uma apresentação de caso segue sempre as mesmas 6 partes. Leia os dois casos abaixo e, para cada parte, fale sobre o Case B dentro do tempo do cronômetro, usando as frases úteis. Se quiser ver como soaria, abra o exemplo com o Case A. Esta aba não vale pontos.

Case A — exemplo

  • 43-year-old woman, no known cardiovascular risk factors
  • Severe epigastric pain → acute dyspnea, central cyanosis, circulatory collapse → shockable cardiac arrest
  • ROSC after ~10 min of CPR
  • Post-arrest ECG: atrial fibrillation, no ischemia
  • Angiography: 50% lesion, mid LAD → type 3 SCAD, confirmed by IVUS
  • Conservative management in the ICU
  • Discharged with no neurological deficits

Case B — você apresenta

  • 56-year-old woman, no previous CV disease, android obesity
  • 3 days of constrictive, intense, prolonged retrosternal chest pain radiating to the left shoulder
  • BP 135/75 mmHg, HR 95 bpm, apyretic
  • ECG: convex ST-segment elevation in anterior leads + Q waves
  • Echo: apical and septoapical akinesia, EF 59%
  • Troponin >1000 times normal
  • Angiography: spontaneous dissection of the LAD, no atheromatous lesion
  • Aspirin + beta-blockers; favorable evolution at 6 weeks
Part 1
Patient presentation
A ...-year-old woman presented with... with no previous history of... risk factors included... pain radiating to...
Introduce the patient: age, sex, risk factors and the main complaint. Where did the pain start and how long did it last?
Exemplo · Case A
"A 43-year-old woman with no known cardiovascular risk factors presented with severe epigastric pain, followed by sudden shortness of breath and central cyanosis."
Part 2
Clinical findings
On examination, ... The ECG revealed... The echocardiography showed... Troponin was elevated / within normal limits
Describe the vital signs, the ECG, the echocardiography and the lab results for Case B.
Exemplo · Case A
"After resuscitation, the ECG showed atrial fibrillation without signs of ischemia. Coronary angiography revealed a 50% lesion in the mid left anterior descending artery, and the ventriculography showed hypokinesia of the anterior wall."
Part 3
Diagnosis & differential
The findings were consistent with... There was no evidence of... The diagnosis was confirmed by... If it were ..., we would expect...
What was the diagnosis? Explain why it was not a typical heart attack. Use a second conditional: "If it were atherosclerosis, we would expect..."
Exemplo · Case A
"The findings were consistent with type 3 spontaneous coronary artery dissection, confirmed by intravascular ultrasound. There was no evidence of atherosclerosis. If it were a typical heart attack, we would expect to see a plaque in the artery."
Part 4
Treatment & management
The patient received... The team opted for conservative management because... Instead of..., they chose... If she became unstable, they would consider...
What treatment did the patient receive, and why? What would the team do if she got worse?
Exemplo · Case A
"The IVUS findings did not support an immediate percutaneous intervention, so the team opted for conservative management in the ICU. If she became unstable, they would consider an intervention."
Part 5
Outcome & follow-up
She was discharged... The outcome was favorable At the 6-week follow-up... She has been recovering well since...
How did the patient evolve? What did the follow-up show? Use the Present Perfect Continuous at least once.
Exemplo · Case A
"The vasopressors were gradually withdrawn, she was extubated early, and she was discharged without neurological deficits. Since then, she has been attending regular check-ups."
Part 6
Take-home message
This case highlights... Clinicians should consider... It is important to remember that... If we recognized it earlier, ... would...
What should other doctors learn from this case? Finish with one conditional sentence.
Exemplo · Case A
"This case highlights that SCAD can cause cardiac arrest in young women without risk factors. If clinicians looked for it earlier, more patients would receive the right diagnosis."
